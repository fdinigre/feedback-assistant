# IA marking calibration notes (Mathematics AI SL — Exploration)

Apply these together with the official Exploration rubric. They capture the
teacher's calibrated standards and are anonymised (no student names or work).

## Language — most IB students are NOT native English speakers
Do not lower any criterion for imperfect grammar, spelling, or phrasing.
- Criterion A (Presentation) rewards coherence, organisation and conciseness —
  achievable regardless of English fluency.
- Criterion B (Mathematical communication) assesses mathematical language,
  notation and representation — NOT English prose.
Penalise language only when it genuinely prevents the mathematics from being
understood. Never let non-native phrasing pull down A or B.

## What each top band looks like (teacher's standards)
- **A (Presentation) = 4:** coherent, well-organised, and genuinely CONCISE (no
  padding — if 20 words suffice, 100 words is not concise; no irrelevant or
  repeated calculations, graphs or descriptions); clear identifiable aim;
  logically developed; all appropriate avenues explored; graphs and tables well
  placed; easy to follow; proper citations/referencing; strong introduction and
  conclusion.
- **B (Mathematical communication) = 4:** key terms and variables explicitly
  defined; correct mathematical language, terminology, symbols and notation;
  appropriate and VARIED forms of representation (formulae, diagrams, tables,
  charts, graphs, models); equations and graphs numbered and titled.
- **C (Personal engagement) = 3 (outstanding):** authentic engagement in
  numerous high-quality instances that drive the exploration creatively; thinks
  creatively; builds models for real-world situations; asks questions, makes
  conjectures, investigates; actively explores and applies UNFAMILIAR /
  beyond-syllabus mathematics — this is REWARDED, not penalised (e.g. using
  volume of revolution even though it is not on the SL syllabus); leaves the
  impression of complete understanding of the topic's context.
- **D (Reflection) = 3 (substantial):** critical reflection THROUGHOUT (crucial,
  deciding, or deeply insightful); addresses results and their impact on the
  student's understanding; consistently considers "what's next"; discusses
  implications, accuracy and reasonableness, and significance of findings;
  discusses limitations and extensions; critically evaluates the choices of
  approach; discusses mathematical difficulties, problems and contradictions.
- **E (Use of mathematics):** follow the rubric's level descriptors. Award 6 when
  the mathematics is correct and rigorous throughout with thorough understanding;
  5 (relevant mathematics commensurate with the course, MOSTLY correct, good
  knowledge and clear logic) is the right band when there are minor gaps in
  correctness or rigour. Do not drop E for language.

## Calibration anchor (worked example, anonymised)
A strong exploration that meets the A/B/C/D standards above and whose mathematics
is mostly (not perfectly) correct is a **19/20 — A:4, B:4, C:3, D:3, E:5**. Mark
real work in that spirit: top bands are reachable and should be awarded when the
evidence supports them; E:5 vs 6 turns on genuine mathematical correctness/rigour,
nothing else.
